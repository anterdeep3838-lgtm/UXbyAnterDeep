26UX laws

UX Laws

The rules of the game.

01

Hick's Law

Fewer choices, faster decisions. Four menu pillars, and one kit question at a time.

02

Fitts's Law

Big, close targets. 52 px pill buttons, and the whole card is the swipe area.

03

Jakob's Law

Familiar patterns. Cart top right, filters on the left, checkout in steps, like the stores people already use.

04

Miller's Law

Small chunks. Compare stops at three, and kits split into core gear and optional extras.

05

Goal Gradient

Visible progress pulls people forward. Kit dots and the checkout stepper show how close the finish is.

06

Von Restorff

What stands out gets noticed. Only the primary action wears the ember gradient.

28Key learnings

Key Learnings

What the race taught me.

  1. 01

    Translate, don't just list

    A spec only helps once it says what it means for the player's game.

  2. 02

    Trust lives in the details

    Full totals, honest "Not listed" labels and clear errors earn more confidence than any banner.

  3. 03

    Guide, never push

    The kit builder suggests and explains, but the shopper always makes the final call.

  4. 04

    Cut to stay fast

    Removing features that did not fit made the core journeys sharper.

How I worked
"AI generated the screens. These rules decided what they were allowed to be."

Five rules I wrote into every prompt.

The interface came from Claude and Codex. What it shows, what it refuses to show and how it behaves came from these rules, quoted from my own iteration prompts. Each one returns later in the case study, at the screen where it was applied.

  1. 01

    Ask only what changes the result.

    From my prompt“Show exactly four onboarding steps before Home… a large bottom sheet with no more than six relevant categories.”

    Hick's lawProgressive disclosureSeen in Onboarding · Home
  2. 02

    AI suggests, the person decides.

    From my prompt“AI must use supported information only and never invent facts, automatically confirm visits, send messages, negotiate independently or decide for users.”

    User controland freedomSeen in Compare
  3. 03

    Prevent the error, don't explain it.

    From my prompt“Compare must be disabled below two selections… reject impossible dates such as 31 February without losing other answers… Sending a message must never automatically confirm a visit.”

    Error preventionNielsen heuristic 5Seen in Compare · Visits
  4. 04

    Familiar beats clever.

    From my prompt“An iOS-style scrolling clock wheel with centered selection, faded surrounding values… two vertically arranged ticks for messages sent by the current user.”

    Jakob's lawMental modelsSeen in Visit request · Inbox
  5. 05

    Never create dead buttons, decorative tabs or fake state changes.

    Every visible control must perform a clear action and return an immediate, understandable state. Tabs, buttons and feedback appear only when they support a real part of the flow.

    Consistencyand standardsSeen in Landlord dashboard
Meet Belong
"Homes, roommates and landlords in one place, from your first search to move-in day."

One app for the whole move.

Where it started
"From one Reddit thread to a product question."

I spoke with people on Reddit who were relocating for a job or college and searching for roommates there. Their stories kept landing on the same three pressures, stacked one on top of the other.

Conversations on RedditPeople relocating for work or study
1Pressure

The move itself is hectic

A new state, a new job or campus, and a deadline to have somewhere to live.

2Pressure

Everything lands on one person

Listings, owner calls, visits and moving logistics, handled alone.

3The breaking point

Then, a roommate on top of it

Someone with the same budget and the same way of living, found through scattered posts.

What I heard, and what I assumed from it

Three things people told me, and the assumption I built each part of Belong on.

What I heardWhat I assumed

Homes come from listing apps. Roommates come from Reddit and group chats.

One search for both means fewer tools to juggle.

Budget isn't enough. Sleep, food and cleanliness decide who you can live with.

Six lifestyle habits are enough to rank roommates usefully.

The pressure peaks in the weeks right before the move.

A move-in date asked up front makes the first results feel relevant.

"Ask only what changes the first results. Everything else can wait."

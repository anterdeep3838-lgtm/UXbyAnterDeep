Worth02 · The problem

Problem& Solution

Worth makes everyday money easy to record, easy to understand and easy to keep within limits.

❯❯❯❯

The problem

Frequent small UPI payments make spending difficult to track. Manual logging takes effort, and budget overruns often go unnoticed, leaving users unsure where their money actually went.

Solution 1 · Record in seconds

A central Add with a calculator keypad, or just say it: voice entry records expenses, income and savings, then waits for a quick review.

Solution 2 · Guided, not surprised

Category budgets alert before a limit is reached, Analysis shows where money went, and Finly AI answers questions about your own spending.

Worth02 · Problem framing

Problem Framing

Five questions that shaped the brief.

How might we

Help people understand their everyday spending and stay within budget without the effort of manual tracking?

  1. Who

    Salaried young professionals in India, roughly 22–35, who pay digitally many times a day.

  2. What

    Logging feels tedious, budgets get checked too late, and spending patterns stay unclear.

  3. Where

    In everyday moments: right after a payment, before a purchase, and when the month closes.

  4. Why

    Without real-time feedback, budgets go unchecked and savings goals slip (CFPB).

  5. How

    Tap or voice entry, category budgets with early alerts, and Analysis plus Finly AI to explain the numbers.

Worth07 · Product evolution

Product Evolution

SpendWise → Worth.

SpendWise worked, but every element spoke at the same volume. Worth gives the answer room and lets the rest support it.

SpendWise · v1Worth · v2

  1. Money Left card

    BeforeA big number and an unlabelled lime bar.

    AfterThe period is named, the bar says “Spent 65%”, and the amount can be hidden.

    Why: a number only helps when you know what it covers.

  2. One accent colour

    BeforePurple and lime competed for attention.

    AfterOne blue for actions; mint and coral only mean income and spending.

    Why: colour should carry meaning, not decoration.

  3. Floating dock

    BeforeA fixed tab bar with a raised + button.

    AfterA slim dock that names the active tab, with Add in the centre.

    Why: more room for content, and Add is always within reach.

  4. Finly insight

    BeforeA one-line tip, cut off mid-sentence.

    AfterA full insight that says it's “based on your transactions”, plus Ask Finly.

    Why: advice is trusted when its source is clear.

Worth07 · Product evolution

What Changed Across the App

What changed across the app.

Five areas, each moved from showing everything at once to showing the next useful thing.

  1. Add transaction

    One long form: amount, eight categories, description, date, payment, note and repeat, all on one page.

    Two calm steps. Amount first on a calculator keypad, then details. Voice entry shows what it heard before saving.

  2. Analysis

    Three stat tiles, a donut and weekly bars, each telling part of the story.

    One answer first. What you spent, the daily average and a fair comparison with the same days last period.

  3. Budget

    Edit and delete icons on every category row.

    Status and pace. An overall gauge, spend per day and clear labels; changes live behind “Edit limit”.

  4. Savings

    Rings with saved, target and remaining for each goal.

    Goals become actions. “Save ₹3,500 a month to finish on time”, right under the progress bar.

  5. Navigation

    A single letter avatar in the corner of Home.

    Everything one tap away. The avatar opens a side menu with Profile, Currency, AI preferences and Security.

Key Design Decisions & Trade-offs

Four choices that shaped Worth.

Each one gave up something small to make recording, understanding and staying on budget easier.

Decision 01

Finly answers from your data

Chose
An assistant that answers only from the user's own transactions.
Instead of
A general chatbot or static money tips.
Trade-off
A narrower scope, but every answer is personal and checkable.

Decision 02

Analysis apart from Home

Chose
Home gives the overview; Analysis explains the month.
Instead of
Charts stacked on Home.
Trade-off
One extra tap for charts, and a much calmer Home.

Decision 03

Voice, with a review step

Chose
Speak one sentence, then check what Worth understood.
Instead of
Saving whatever was heard straight away.
Trade-off
One extra tap, and no silently wrong entries.

Decision 04

Alert before the limit

Chose
Alerts at 70, 80 or 90% of each budget, plus a safe daily pace.
Instead of
A warning only once a limit breaks.
Trade-off
More notifications, so users choose the threshold.
Worth14 · Iterations

UX Approach & Iterations

Three iterations, up close.

Where I started, what I noticed, what I changed, and why the new version works better.

Iteration 01 · Add transaction

  1. StartedOne long form with every field on one page.
  2. NoticedThe amount, the one field that matters, competed with eight others; Save sat below the fold.
  3. ChangedAmount first on a keypad, then details with smart defaults like Today and UPI.
  4. BecauseOne decision per step is faster and leads to fewer mistakes.

Iteration 02 · Money Left card

  1. StartedA big number, two tiles and an unlabelled lime bar.
  2. NoticedThe card didn't say which dates it covered or what the bar measured.
  3. ChangedA date chip, a “Spent 65%” legend, a comparison line and a hide button.
  4. BecauseA number is only trusted when its context is visible.

Iteration 03 · Budget

  1. StartedEdit and delete icons on every category row.
  2. NoticedChanges sat right next to everyday reading, and status was only a bar.
  3. ChangedAn overall gauge with On track, Approaching or Over, a daily pace, and edits behind “Edit limit”.
  4. BecauseReading should feel safe; changes should be deliberate.
WorthUX laws

UX Laws in Practice

Six principles, built into the screens.

Each law is shown where it lives in the Worth prototype.

Postel's law

What it is: Accept flexible input, but return a clear and consistent result.
In Worth: Voice accepts phrases like “about 2k”, then presents a structured result to review.

Fitts's law

What it is: Important actions should be large and easy to reach.
In Worth: Add is the dock's largest target and sits directly under the thumb.

Hick's law

What it is: Fewer, clearer choices reduce decision time.
In Worth: Eight distinct icon categories keep classification quick and scannable.

Tesler's law

What it is: Necessary complexity should be handled by the product where possible.
In Worth: The keypad calculates split bills so users do not need to work them out elsewhere.

Goal-gradient effect

What it is: Motivation increases as progress toward a goal becomes visible.
In Worth: Progress bars and a monthly saving amount make each goal feel achievable.

Peak-end rule

What it is: People remember the strongest moment and the ending of an experience.
In Worth: Every entry ends with a clear receipt that confirms exactly what was saved.
